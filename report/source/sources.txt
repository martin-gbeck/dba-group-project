SOURCE CARDS: DBA3803 group report
Cite only these cards. Each card gives the full reference, the one claim the report may cite it for, and what it does not support. Every card was checked with a web search on 2026-11-14.

[1] Lago, J., Marcjasz, G., De Schutter, B. and Weron, R. (2021). Forecasting day-ahead electricity prices: A review of state-of-the-art algorithms, best practices and an open-access benchmark. Applied Energy, 293, 116983. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2021.116983
Claim it backs: LEAR (Lasso Estimated AutoRegressive, one Lasso per delivery hour on lagged prices and exogenous forecasts) is one of the two open-access benchmark models the paper proposes for day-ahead price forecasting, and the paper argues new methods should be compared against such well-performing simple benchmarks. In the paper LEAR is recalibrated every day.
Checked: abstract (arXiv 2008.08004) says the field lacks rigorous evaluation and new methods are rarely compared with well-performing simpler benchmarks; LEAR and its daily recalibration are defined in the body of the paper, not in the abstract.
Does not support: anything about XGBoost or Lasso-then-XGBoost; any claim about the German market in 2023 to 2025; any claim that LEAR is the best model in general (the paper finds a deep neural network more accurate on average).

[2] Chen, T. and Guestrin, C. (2016). XGBoost: A scalable tree boosting system. In Proceedings of the 22nd ACM SIGKDD International Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining (pp. 785-794). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/2939672.2939785
Claim it backs: XGBoost is a gradient tree boosting system: it adds regression trees one at a time, each fitted to what the trees before it got wrong.
Does not support: any claim about electricity prices, or about combining a Lasso with XGBoost.

[3] Tibshirani, R. (1996). Regression shrinkage and selection via the lasso. Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B (Methodological), 58(1), 267-288. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1996.tb02080.x
Claim it backs: the Lasso is linear regression with a penalty on the absolute size of the coefficients, which shrinks some coefficients and sets others exactly to zero.
Does not support: anything about forecasting, trees or the two-stage model.

[4] Hyndman, R. J. and Koehler, A. B. (2006). Another look at measures of forecast accuracy. International Journal of Forecasting, 22(4), 679-688. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2006.03.001
Claim it backs: the mean absolute scaled error (MASE) scales a forecast's MAE by the MAE of a naive forecast, so errors can be compared across series at different levels; the paper proposes it as a standard measure.
Does not support: the choice of same hour yesterday as the naive forecast (that is our choice; the paper uses the one-step naive forecast in-sample); anything about decision-based scoring.

[5] Künsch, H. R. (1989). The jackknife and the bootstrap for general stationary observations. The Annals of Statistics, 17(3), 1217-1241. https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176347265
Claim it backs: the moving-block bootstrap: resample blocks of consecutive observations with replacement, so dependence between neighbouring observations is kept.
Does not support: the specific block lengths of 1, 7 and 30 days (our choice); anything about electricity.

[6] Elmachtoub, A. N. and Grigas, P. (2022). Smart "predict, then optimize". Management Science, 68(1), 9-26. https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3922
Claim it backs: when a forecast feeds a decision, the loss that matters is the cost of the decision the forecast leads to, which can differ from the forecast's prediction error; the paper measures a forecast by that decision error.
Does not support: any claim about electricity, shift scheduling or XGBoost; we do not use its SPO+ training loss, only its argument for scoring forecasts by the decision.

[7] Arbeitszeitgesetz (ArbZG, German Working Hours Act), section 5(1) Ruhezeit. https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbzg/__5.html
Claim it backs: after the end of the daily working time, employees must have an uninterrupted rest period of at least 11 hours.
Does not support: the exceptions (hospitals, restaurants, on-call work, collective agreements under section 7 that can shorten rest by up to two hours); anything about night or weekend pay premiums.

[8] Commission Regulation (EU) No 543/2013 of 14 June 2013 on submission and publication of data in electricity markets, Articles 6(2)(b) and 14(2)(d). https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2013/543
Claim it backs: TSOs must publish the day-ahead total load forecast no later than two hours before day-ahead gate closure (Art. 6(2)(b)), and the day-ahead wind and solar generation forecast no later than 18:00 Brussels time on the day before delivery (Art. 14(2)(d)). They must also publish a week-ahead load forecast, a maximum and minimum for each day, every Friday (Art. 6(1)(c), 6(2)(c)); Article 14 has no week-ahead wind or solar forecast.
Does not support: when TSOs compute their forecasts (TenneT's own statement, in project.md, that it estimates wind at 08:00, is not in this regulation); anything about commercial vendor forecasts.

[9] Single Day-Ahead Coupling (SDAC) gate closure: ACER, REMIT reporting guidance, question 2.1.29 (https://www.acer.europa.eu/question-2129-data-field-30), and Nord Pool, SDAC 15-minute MTU go-live notice (https://support.nordpoolgroup.com/support/solutions/articles/8000121859-about-sdac-15-minute-member-test).
Claim it backs: the European day-ahead auction closes at 12:00 CET for delivery from 00:00 to 24:00 the next day, and the market moved to 15-minute products with first delivery on 1 October 2025.
Does not support: the exact time results are published (sources differ, roughly 12:45 to 13:00 on a normal day).

[10] Fraunhofer ISE, Energy-Charts API. https://api.energy-charts.info
Claim it backs: the source of all data in the project (DE-LU day-ahead prices, neighbouring zone prices, TSO day-ahead forecasts of load, solar and wind), public, no registration, licensed CC BY 4.0 unless a response states otherwise.
Does not support: any statement about forecast quality or publication times.

[11] energy & meteo systems (n.d.). Market forecasts for wind and solar power in Europe. Product page. https://www.emsys-renewables.com/products/power_forecasts/market-forecasts-wind-solar.php
Claim it backs: a commercial vendor sells wind and solar power forecasts for European markets, Germany included, with a horizon of up to ten days.
Checked: page read 2026-11-14; it states "a time horizon of up to ten days" and offers Germany-specific feed-in forecasts. No date on the page.
Does not support: the accuracy of those forecasts; their price; that any TSO publishes such a forecast; anything about whether they would let a model beat the last-week rule.
